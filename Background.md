Computer vision systems observe projections, depth samples, or silhouettes and infer a latent body state. The simplest latent state is a skeleton: a graph of joints with estimated positions or rotations. More expressive states add a body surface, shape parameters, temporal dynamics, or internal landmarks.

This is an inverse problem. Many 3D bodies can produce similar 2D evidence, and even depth cameras are noisy and incomplete. Models supply priors that make the inverse problem solvable, but those priors can also bias the answer.

A useful formal distinction is between **state** and **observation**. The state might contain joint rotations, shape coefficients, surface vertices, or vertebral landmarks. The observation is the image, depth map, or point cloud generated by that state. Estimation seeks a state whose projected evidence agrees with the observation while remaining plausible under the model.
